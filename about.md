    <div class="prose"><p>I am a Postdoctoral Associate at the <a href="https://www.hgsc.bcm.edu/">Human Genome Sequencing Center</a> at Baylor College of Medicine, working with <a href="https://fritzsedlazeck.github.io/">Fritz Sedlazeck</a>. I develop computational methods for long-read sequencing, with a focus on genetic variation, DNA methylation, haplotype phasing, and cellular context.</p><p>My work brings together algorithm development and biological interpretation. I am particularly interested in how the information carried on a single DNA molecule can help us understand complex variation, from tandem repeats in different cell populations to variants implicated in rare disease.</p><p>I earned my Ph.D. in Computer Science at <a href="https://cs.rice.edu/">Rice University</a>, advised by <a href="https://www.treangenlab.com/">Todd Treangen</a> and Fritz Sedlazeck. Before Rice, I studied computer science at <a href="https://en.hit.edu.cn/">Harbin Institute of Technology</a> and spent a semester as an exchange student at UC Berkeley.</p><p>This site is also home to my <a href="{{ '/notes/' | relative_url }}">notes, talks, and occasional personal writing</a>.</p></div>
